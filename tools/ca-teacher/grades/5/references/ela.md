# Grade 5 English Language Arts (California Common Core) - paraphrased

Official text: https://www.cde.ca.gov/be/st/ss/

## Reading: Literature (RL.5)
- **RL.5.1** Quote accurately from a text when explaining what it says explicitly and when drawing inferences.
- **RL.5.2** Determine a theme from details, including how characters respond to challenges or how a speaker in a poem reflects on a topic; summarize the text.
- **RL.5.3** Compare and contrast two or more characters, settings, or events, drawing on specific details.
- **RL.5.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases, including figurative language such as metaphors and similes.
- **RL.5.5** Explain how a series of chapters, scenes, or stanzas fits together to provide the overall structure.
- **RL.5.6** Describe how a narrator's or speaker's point of view influences how events are described.
- **RL.5.7** Analyze how visual and multimedia elements contribute to the meaning, tone, or beauty of a text (graphic novel, multimedia presentation).
- **RL.5.9** Compare and contrast stories in the same genre on their approaches to similar themes and topics.
- **RL.5.10** By year's end, read and comprehend grade 4-5 literature independently and proficiently.

## Reading: Informational Text (RI.5)
- **RI.5.1** Quote accurately from a text when explaining what it says explicitly and when drawing inferences.
- **RI.5.2** Determine two or more main ideas and explain how they are supported by key details; summarize the text.
- **RI.5.3** Explain the relationships or interactions between two or more individuals, events, ideas, or concepts in a historical, scientific, or technical text.
- **RI.5.4** Determine the meaning of general academic and domain-specific words.
- **RI.5.5** Compare and contrast the overall structure (chronology, comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution) of events, ideas, or information in two or more texts.
- **RI.5.6** Analyze multiple accounts of the same event or topic, noting important similarities and differences in point of view.
- **RI.5.7** Draw on information from multiple print or digital sources to locate an answer quickly or solve a problem efficiently.
- **RI.5.8** Explain how an author uses reasons and evidence to support particular points, identifying which reasons and evidence support which points.
- **RI.5.9** Integrate information from several texts on the same topic to write or speak knowledgeably.
- **RI.5.10** By year's end, read and comprehend grade 4-5 informational texts independently and proficiently.

## Foundational Skills (RF.5)
- **RF.5.3** Use combined knowledge of letter-sound correspondences, syllabication patterns, and morphology (roots and affixes) to read unfamiliar multisyllabic words accurately.
- **RF.5.4** Read with sufficient accuracy and fluency to support comprehension, using context to confirm or self-correct.

## Writing (W.5)
- **W.5.1** Opinion pieces: introduce a topic clearly, state an opinion, create a logical organizational structure, provide logically ordered reasons supported by facts and details, link with words, phrases, and clauses (consequently, specifically), and provide a concluding section.
- **W.5.2** Informative texts: introduce a topic with a general observation and focus, group related information logically, include formatting and multimedia when useful, develop with facts, definitions, details, quotations, and examples, link ideas across categories (in contrast, especially), use precise and domain-specific language, and provide a conclusion.
- **W.5.3** Narratives: orient the reader, organize a natural event sequence, use narrative techniques (dialogue, description, pacing), use transitional words, phrases, and clauses, use concrete words and sensory details, and provide a conclusion.
- **W.5.4-9** Produce clear, coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose, and audience; plan, revise, edit, rewrite, or try a new approach; use technology to publish and collaborate (including keyboarding two pages in one sitting); conduct short research projects using several sources; summarize or paraphrase information in notes and finished work, with a list of sources; draw evidence from literary or informational texts.
- **W.5.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames.

## Speaking & Listening (SL.5)
- **SL.5.1** Collaborative discussions: come prepared, follow rules and carry out roles, pose and respond to specific questions by making comments that contribute and elaborate, and review key ideas and draw conclusions.
- **SL.5.2-3** Summarize a written text read aloud or information presented in diverse media; summarize the points a speaker makes and explain how each claim is supported by reasons and evidence.
- **SL.5.4-6** Report on a topic or text or present an opinion, sequencing ideas logically with appropriate facts and details, speaking clearly at an understandable pace; include multimedia components and visual displays; adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, using formal English when appropriate.

## Language (L.5)
- **L.5.1** Grammar: explain the function of conjunctions, prepositions, and interjections; form and use perfect verb tenses; use verb tense to convey various times, sequences, states, and conditions; recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in verb tense; use correlative conjunctions (either/or, neither/nor).
- **L.5.2** Conventions: punctuation for items in a series; a comma to separate an introductory element; commas to set off yes/no, tag questions, and direct address; underlining, quotation marks, or italics for titles of works; spell grade-appropriate words correctly, consulting references.
- **L.5.3** Expand, combine, and reduce sentences for meaning, reader interest, and style; compare and contrast varieties of English (dialects, registers) used in stories, dramas, or poems.
- **L.5.4-6** Determine meanings using context, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and reference materials; interpret figurative language, including similes and metaphors; recognize and explain idioms, adages, and proverbs; use synonyms, antonyms, and homographs; acquire and use grade-appropriate academic and domain-specific words, including words that signal contrast, addition, and other logical relationships.
