# Grade 5 History-Social Science (California) - paraphrased

Theme: **United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation.** Students study the development of the nation up to 1850, with emphasis on the people who were already here, when and why others came, and the founding of the United States.

Official text: https://www.cde.ca.gov/be/st/ss/

- **5.1** Describe the major pre-Columbian settlements, including the cliff dwellers and pueblo people of the desert Southwest, the American Indians of the Pacific Northwest, the nomadic nations of the Great Plains, and the woodland peoples east of the Mississippi River: how geography and climate shaped their ways of life, their varied customs and economies, and their systems of government.
- **5.2** Trace the routes of early explorers and describe the early explorations of the Americas: the aims, obstacles, and accomplishments of the explorers and sponsors; the technology that made exploration possible; and the impact of exploration on American Indians and the land.
- **5.3** Describe the cooperation and conflict that existed among American Indians and between the Indian nations and the new settlers: competition for land, trade, cultural exchanges, alliances, wars, broken treaties, and the impact of disease.
- **5.4** Understand the political, religious, social, and economic institutions that evolved in the colonial era: the influence of location and physical setting on founding the original colonies; the major individuals and groups responsible for founding them; religious aspects of the colonies; self-government in the colonies; the introduction of slavery into America, the experiences of enslaved Africans, and the development of the institution; and the economic patterns of the colonies.
- **5.5** Explain the causes of the American Revolution: the events and laws that led to colonial resistance to British rule, the significance of the First and Second Continental Congresses and the Committees of Correspondence, and the people and ideas that shaped the Declaration of Independence.
- **5.6** Understand the course and consequences of the American Revolution: key battles, leaders, and turning points; the contributions of France and other nations; the roles of women, African Americans, and American Indians during the Revolution; and the economic and political impact of the war, including the Articles of Confederation.
- **5.7** Describe the people and events associated with the development of the U.S. Constitution and analyze its significance as the foundation of the American republic: the shortcomings of the Articles of Confederation, the Constitutional Convention and its compromises, the Bill of Rights, the powers of government and the separation of powers, and the meaning of the American creed that calls on citizens to safeguard the liberty of individual Americans.
- **5.8** Trace the colonization, immigration, and settlement patterns of the American people from 1789 to the mid-1800s, including the routes of westward expansion, the experiences of settlers, and the impact on American Indian nations, including forced removal.
- **5.9** Know the location of the current 50 states and the names of their capitals.

## Teaching notes
- Use primary sources and multiple perspectives, including those of American Indians, enslaved and free African Americans, and women.
- Avoid simulations or role-plays of enslavement or other atrocities; use primary sources and discussion instead.
- Cover American Indian nations as diverse and as present-day sovereign nations, not only in the past.
