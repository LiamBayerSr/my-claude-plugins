# Grade 3 History-Social Science (California) - paraphrased

Theme: **Continuity and Change.** Students study their local community: its geography, the American Indian nations of the region, its history and settlement, government, and economy.

Official text: https://www.cde.ca.gov/be/st/ss/

- **3.1** Describe the physical and human geography and use maps, tables, graphs, photographs, and charts to organize information about people, places, and environments in a spatial context: identify geographical features in the local region (deserts, mountains, valleys, hills, coastal areas, oceans, lakes) and trace the ways people have used the resources of the local region and modified the physical environment.
- **3.2** Describe the American Indian nations in the local region long ago and in the recent past: national identities, religious beliefs, customs, and folklore traditions; how physical geography (climate, landforms, natural resources) shaped their housing, clothing, tools, and food; their economic activities, legends, and trade; and their governance structures, including the current status of tribal nations.
- **3.3** Draw from historical and community resources to organize the sequence of local historical events and describe how each period of settlement left its mark on the land: research explorers who visited the area, identify the American Indian nations and successive settlers and their effects, and trace why the community was established, how people and institutions changed, and who the important figures were.
- **3.4** Understand the role of rules and laws in daily lives and the basic structure of US government: why we have rules, how laws are made, and why it matters to follow them; the role of citizens including civic participation; the three branches of government at the local, state, and federal levels; and important historical figures who have demonstrated civic leadership. Also know the histories of important local and national landmarks, symbols, and essential documents that create a sense of community among citizens and exemplify cherished ideals (the flag, the bald eagle, the Statue of Liberty, the Constitution).
- **3.5** Demonstrate basic economic reasoning skills and an understanding of the economy of the local region: describe how local producers have used and are using natural, human, and capital resources; understand that some goods are made locally and others are imported; understand that individual economic choices involve trade-offs; and discuss the relationship of students' "work" in school and personal human capital.

## Teaching notes
- Make 3.2 and 3.3 local: research which tribal nations' lands the school is on, using tribal sources where possible.
- Teach American Indian nations as living communities with present-day governments, not only as history. Avoid costume, dress-up, and craft activities that stereotype.
