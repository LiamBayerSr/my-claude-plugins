Plan a California {{ADJ}} lesson for: $ARGUMENTS

Use the `{{TEACH}}` skill for lesson design, curriculum, and templates, and the `{{STD}}` skill to choose and cite standards.

## 1. Work out what you have

Check the request and the conversation (including attached files and Project files) for three optional inputs. Every combination is valid; never require one the teacher didn't give.

- **What to teach** - a topic, standard code, or curriculum lesson name. If there is none and no curriculum pages either, ask one short question: "What lesson or topic?"
- **Curriculum material** - photos, a PDF, pasted text, or a description of the lesson from the teacher's program (HMH or any other). If present, follow "Working from adopted curriculum." If absent, design the lesson from the standards. Never invent what a named program's lesson contains; if the teacher names a program lesson without sharing it, either ask for the pages or build from the standard and say that's what you did.
- **Template** - a lesson plan template the teacher must use (document, photo, or pasted outline). If present, follow "Using the teacher's template." If absent, use the default format below.

Assume {{MINUTES}} minutes if no length was given. If the teacher asked for a week, start with a short table (day, lesson, objective, practice), then one compact plan per day.

## 2. Default format (when no template was given)

1. **Standards** - primary code(s) with a one-line paraphrase; supporting standards; Math Practice or CA NGSS practice if relevant; ELD focus.
2. **Learning target** - "I can..." in kid language, and a success criterion the teacher can observe.
3. **Materials** - the curriculum's pages and manipulatives, or common classroom items.
4. **Vocabulary** - 3-5 words with kid-friendly definitions.
5. **Lesson sequence** - hook, I do, we do, you do, with minutes for each and key think-aloud lines. With curriculum, keep its activities and use its worksheet as the "you do," citing pages ("TE p. 112").
6. **Check for understanding** - the curriculum's own check if it has one, otherwise a 2-3 item exit ticket with answers.
7. **Differentiation** - support and extension, plus one sentence frame each for Emerging, Expanding, and Bridging English learners.
8. **Teacher notes** - likely misconceptions and timing risks.
9. **Family connection** - one optional, low-cost at-home activity.

Keep each lesson to about two printed pages (one page per day for a week).

## 3. Finish

In one line, say what you used: "Built from your HMH pages and your template" or "Built from standard {{EXAMPLE_CODE}} using the default format." Then offer a leveled worksheet (`/differentiate` or `/worksheet`) or a family note (`/parent-email`).
