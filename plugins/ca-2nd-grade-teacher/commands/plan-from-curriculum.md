Build a lesson plan from the teacher's adopted curriculum materials for: $ARGUMENTS

Follow the "Working from adopted curriculum" section of the `second-grade-teaching` skill, and use the `ca-grade2-standards` skill to confirm standards.

1. **Get the source.** If no pages, photos, PDF, or pasted text are in the conversation, ask the teacher to attach the teacher's-edition pages and the student worksheet for this lesson (photos from a phone are fine). Do not invent what the curriculum says.
2. **Read it first.** Identify the curriculum's stated lesson objective, standards, vocabulary, activities, and the worksheet's skills. If something is unreadable, say which part.
3. **If the teacher asked for a week**, make a short table first (day, curriculum lesson, objective, practice page), then one compact plan per day. Otherwise, plan one lesson.

For each lesson, produce:
- **Standards** - the curriculum's standards, checked against the California codes; flag any mismatch politely.
- **Learning target** - "I can..." and an observable success criterion.
- **Lesson sequence with minutes** - hook, I do, we do, you do. Keep the curriculum's core activities and routines; summarize them in your own words with a page reference ("TE p. 112") rather than copying text. Use the curriculum's student worksheet as the "you do" practice.
- **Check for understanding** - use the curriculum's own check if it has one; otherwise add a 2-3 item exit ticket.
- **ELD supports** - one sentence frame each for Emerging, Expanding, and Bridging, tied to the lesson's vocabulary.
- **Differentiation** - how to support students who struggle with the worksheet and extend for those who finish early, without making a whole new page unless asked.
- **Teacher notes** - likely misconceptions and timing risks.

Keep each daily plan to about one printed page. End by offering a leveled version of the worksheet (`/differentiate`) or a family note about what the class is learning (`/parent-email`).
