# Wage and hour basics (California)

Check the applicable Industrial Welfare Commission (IWC) wage order for the industry; details vary.

## Overtime (Labor Code §510)
- 1.5× the regular rate for hours over 8 in a workday or 40 in a workweek, and for the first 8 hours on the seventh consecutive workday in a workweek.
- 2× the regular rate for hours over 12 in a workday, and over 8 on the seventh consecutive workday.
- The regular rate includes most nondiscretionary bonuses, commissions, and shift differentials.
- Alternative workweek schedules require a secret-ballot election and filing with DIR.

## Meal and rest periods (Labor Code §§226.7, 512; wage orders)
- **Meal:** an unpaid, duty-free 30-minute meal period starting before the end of the 5th hour of work; a second before the end of the 10th hour. The first can be waived by mutual consent if the workday is 6 hours or less; the second if the workday is 12 hours or less and the first was not waived.
- **Rest:** a paid, duty-free 10-minute rest period per 4 hours worked or major fraction thereof, in the middle of each work period where practicable.
- **Premium:** one additional hour of pay at the regular rate of compensation for each workday a compliant meal period is missed, and one for rest periods.

## Pay timing and final pay (Labor Code §§201-204)
- Most employees must be paid at least twice a month on designated paydays.
- **Termination or layoff:** all final wages, including accrued unused vacation/PTO, due immediately at separation.
- **Resignation with at least 72 hours' notice:** due on the last day.
- **Resignation without notice:** due within 72 hours.
- **Waiting time penalties (§203):** up to 30 days of wages for willful late payment.
- Accrued vacation/PTO is earned wages and cannot be forfeited ("use it or lose it" is prohibited; a reasonable accrual cap is allowed). Sick leave under the paid sick leave law need not be paid out unless it's combined into a PTO policy.

## Wage statements (Labor Code §226)
Each itemized statement must show gross wages, total hours worked (non-exempt), piece-rate units, all deductions, net wages, pay period dates, employee name and last four digits of SSN or an employee ID, employer legal name and address, and all applicable hourly rates with hours at each. Paid sick leave available must also be shown (on the statement or a separate writing on payday).

## Expense reimbursement (Labor Code §2802)
Employers must reimburse necessary business expenses, including a reasonable share of personal cell phone and home internet used for work and mileage.

## Recordkeeping
Keep payroll records at least 3 years (4 years is common practice given the limitations period for some claims). Employees may inspect personnel records (§1198.5, which since 2026 includes education and training records) and payroll records (§226).
