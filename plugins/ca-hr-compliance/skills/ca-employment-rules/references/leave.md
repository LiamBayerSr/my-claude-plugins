# Leave and protected time off (California)
\nVerified November 2026 against the Labor Code, Government Code, and DIR and Civil Rights Department guidance.

Headcount thresholds count all employees, including those outside California for some laws. Confirm the employer's count.

## Paid sick leave (Labor Code §§245-249) - all employers
- Employees who work 30+ days in a year for an employer in California are covered.
- Minimum: 40 hours or 5 days per year (whichever is greater), by accrual (at least 1 hour per 30 worked) or front-loading.
- Accrual cap may be no lower than 80 hours or 10 days; carryover required under accrual.
- Uses include the employee's or a family member's illness, preventive care, and safe-time purposes for victims of certain crimes; employees may designate a family member. Local ordinances (for example, Los Angeles, San Francisco) may require more.
- Not required to be paid at separation unless part of a combined PTO policy.

## California Family Rights Act (CFRA, Gov. Code §12945.2) - 5+ employees
- Up to 12 weeks of job-protected leave in a 12-month period for the employee's serious health condition, bonding with a new child, a family member's serious health condition (including a designated person, grandparent, grandchild, sibling, domestic partner), and certain military exigencies.
- Eligibility: 12 months of service and 1,250 hours in the prior 12 months.
- Federal FMLA applies at 50+ employees; the two may run concurrently in some cases but differ (for example, pregnancy disability).

## Pregnancy Disability Leave (PDL, Gov. Code §12945) - 5+ employees
- Up to 4 months of job-protected leave per pregnancy for disability due to pregnancy, childbirth, or related conditions, from day one of employment. Separate from and in addition to CFRA baby-bonding leave.
- Employers must also provide reasonable accommodation and continue group health coverage.

## Bereavement leave (Gov. Code §12945.7) - 5+ employees
- Up to 5 days within 3 months of the death of a family member; may be unpaid unless the employer's policy provides pay (employees may use accrued vacation or sick leave).
- **Starting January 1, 2027 (SB 1149):** covers the death of a "designated person." Employers may limit employees to one designated person per 12-month period.

## Reproductive loss leave (Gov. Code §12945.6) - 5+ employees
- Up to 5 days following a failed adoption, failed surrogacy, miscarriage, stillbirth, or unsuccessful assisted reproduction; up to 20 days in 12 months for multiple events.

## Other protected time off (common examples)
- **Jury and witness duty:** protected; since 2026 (AB 406), additional protections and paid sick leave use for crime victims attending certain proceedings.
- **Crime victim leave and accommodations:** moved into FEHA (Gov. Code §12945.8).
- **Voting:** up to 2 hours paid if the employee lacks sufficient time outside work.
- **School activities and child care emergencies:** 25+ employees at one location, up to 40 hours a year (8 per month).
- **Military and military spouse leave, volunteer firefighter and emergency responder leave, organ and bone marrow donor leave, lactation accommodation:** check the specific statute.

## State paid leave benefits (wage replacement, not job protection)
- State Disability Insurance (SDI) and Paid Family Leave (PFL) through EDD. PFL will cover care for a designated person starting July 1, 2028 (SB 590).
