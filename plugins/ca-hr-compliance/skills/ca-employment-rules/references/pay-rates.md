# Pay rates and exempt thresholds

Verified November 2026. Sources: California Department of Industrial Relations (DIR) News Release 2026-66 and DIR minimum wage and exemption pages; employer law firm summaries citing them.

## California statewide

| Item | 2026 | 2027 (effective Jan 1, 2027) |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum wage (all employers, regardless of size) | $16.90/hour | $17.40/hour |
| Exempt salary threshold (administrative, executive, professional) - 2× minimum wage for full-time | $70,304/year ($1,352/week) | $72,384/year ($6,032/month; $1,392/week) |
| Computer software professional exemption | $122,573.13/year | $61.03/hour; $10,592.37/month; $127,108.34/year |

Meeting the salary threshold alone never makes an employee exempt. The duties test and other requirements of the exemption must also be met.

## Industry-specific minimums (verify current rates before use)

- **Fast food (AB 1228):** $20.00/hour for covered limited-service restaurants (national chains with 60+ establishments), effective April 2024. The Fast Food Council can adjust it; check DIR before relying on the rate.
- **Health care workers (SB 525):** a schedule of higher minimum wages that varies by facility type, size, and location, phasing up over several years. Use DIR's health care minimum wage page for the exact rate for a facility.
- **H-2A agricultural (AB 2646):** $19.75/hour base starting January 1, 2027, adjusted annually, with rules for corresponding California workers.

## Local minimum wages

Dozens of cities and counties set higher minimums, many adjusting January 1 and some July 1 (for example, City of Los Angeles $18.42 and unincorporated Los Angeles County $18.47 as of July 1, 2026). Always check the ordinance for each work location; the highest applicable rate controls.

## Federal comparison

- Federal minimum wage: $7.25/hour (California's rate controls).
- Federal FLSA white-collar salary threshold: $684/week ($35,568/year) after the 2024 increase was vacated in court. California's threshold is far higher and controls for state law.
- Federal law has no daily overtime; California does.
