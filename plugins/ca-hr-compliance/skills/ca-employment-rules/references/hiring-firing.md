# Hiring, onboarding, and separation (California)

## Hiring restrictions
- **Pay transparency (Labor Code §432.3):** employers with 15+ employees must include a pay scale in job postings (since 2026, a good-faith estimate of the range the employer reasonably expects to pay); all employers must give the pay scale for the position to an applicant who asks and to current employees for their own position. Keep job title and wage history records.
- **Salary history ban:** employers may not ask about or rely on an applicant's salary history.
- **Fair Chance Act (Gov. Code §12952) - 5+ employees:** no questions about conviction history before a conditional offer; an individualized assessment and written notice process is required before rescinding an offer.
- **Off-duty cannabis use (Gov. Code §12954):** no discrimination for off-duty, off-site use or for non-psychoactive drug test results; exceptions for certain positions.
- **Immigration-related practices:** no unfair immigration-related practices or document abuse; since 2027 (AB 2495), protections extend to applicants and former employees.

## Required notices at hire (common examples)
- Wage Theft Prevention Act notice (Labor Code §2810.5) for non-exempt employees, with pay rates, payday, employer and insurer information, and paid sick leave terms.
- State Disability Insurance (DE 2515) and Paid Family Leave (DE 2511) pamphlets.
- CRD sexual harassment prevention information (or an equivalent policy).
- Workers' compensation information.
- Workplace Know Your Rights notice (SB 294), plus the option to designate an emergency contact.
- Federal Form I-9 within three business days of start; California restricts using E-Verify on applicants before an offer.

## Restrictive covenants
- Non-compete agreements are void and unlawful in California (Business and Professions Code §16600 et seq.), including those signed out of state; employers had to notify affected current and former employees by February 14, 2024.
- **Stay-or-pay provisions (AB 692, effective 2026):** generally prohibits requiring workers to repay costs (training, bonuses) if they leave, for agreements entered on or after January 1, 2026, with narrow exceptions. **AB 1697** amended this: existing agreements must be brought into compliance by January 1, 2027, and some retention and discretionary bonus repayment terms may fit the exception even if not signed at the start of employment. Have counsel review any repayment terms.

## Separation
- **Final pay:** see wage-hour.md (immediate on termination; timing for resignations).
- **Required documents:** written notice of change in employment status (Labor Code §1089 / EDD notice), the EDD "For Your Benefit" pamphlet (DE 2320), and COBRA/Cal-COBRA notices as applicable. Notice of the employee's right to request personnel records is common practice.
- **Cal-WARN (Labor Code §1400 et seq.) - covered establishments with 75+ employees:** 60 days' notice of a mass layoff (50+ employees), relocation, or termination. Since 2026, notices must address workforce services; starting January 1, 2027 (SB 951), notices for layoffs caused in whole or substantial part by AI or automation must describe the technology and the job functions automated.
- **Automated decisions (SB 947, effective July 1, 2027):** discipline or termination may not be based solely on an automated decision system; a human must review the decision and the employee must get written notice of ADS use.
- **Release agreements:** releases of claims for employees 40+ need ADEA/OWBPA language and timing; California limits confidentiality and non-disparagement terms about unlawful acts (Gov. Code §12964.5, CCP §1001). Have counsel draft.
