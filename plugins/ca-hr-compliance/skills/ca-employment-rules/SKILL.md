# California employment rules

California generally gives employees more protection than federal law, and the employer must follow whichever rule is more protective. Many California cities and counties set higher minimum wages and their own leave rules.

## Rules for using this reference

1. **Name the effective date.** Pay rates and thresholds change every January 1 (and some local rates on July 1). Always say which year a figure applies to.
2. **Check the employer's size and location.** Many laws apply only above a headcount (5, 15, 25, 50, 75, or 100 employees). Ask for headcount and work locations when the answer depends on them, and flag local ordinances (for example, Los Angeles, San Francisco, San Jose, San Diego, Oakland).
3. **Verify anything that may have changed.** If a figure or law isn't in these files, or the question involves a very recent change, look it up on official sources (Department of Industrial Relations at dir.ca.gov, Civil Rights Department at calcivilrights.ca.gov, EDD at edd.ca.gov, Cal/OSHA, and the legislature at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov) and cite them.
4. **Cite the law.** Labor Code section, Government Code section (FEHA and CFRA), wage order, or bill number.
