# Federal tax figures

Tax numbers change every year and sometimes mid-year. Treat this file set as a starting point, not the last word.

## Rules

1. **Always name the tax year.** "For tax year 2026, the standard deduction for married filing jointly is $32,200." Never give a figure without its year.
2. **Check the year asked about.** If the needed year's figures are not in `references/`, or the reference marks a figure as projected or "verify," look it up on IRS.gov (newsroom releases, the annual inflation-adjustment Revenue Procedure, or the relevant publication) before answering, and cite what you used. If you can't verify, say the figure is unconfirmed.
3. **Prefer primary sources.** Internal Revenue Code, Treasury regulations, Revenue Procedures and Rulings, IRS publications and form instructions. Use secondary sources only to find primary ones.
4. **Watch effective dates.** Many 2025 law changes are temporary (several deductions run 2025-2028; the higher SALT cap reverts after 2029). State the window when it matters.
5. **Federal only.** State rules differ; say so when a question has a state angle and suggest checking the state revenue agency.

## Files

- `references/tax-year-2026.md` - verified 2026 figures (returns filed in 2027)
- `references/tax-year-2027.md` - 2027 figures (returns filed in 2028); retirement limits and SALT cap still pending as of Nov 2026
- `references/obbba-2025-changes.md` - One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025) individual provisions and their effective years
- `references/calendar.md` - recurring filing and payment deadlines

## Staying current

Each fall the IRS publishes the next year's inflation adjustments (usually October-November) and retirement plan limits (usually November). Each plugin update adds a new `tax-year-YYYY.md` file. If a user asks about a year with no file, search for the official release rather than extrapolating.
