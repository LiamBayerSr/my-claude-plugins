# Grade 4 History-Social Science (California) - paraphrased

Theme: **California: A Changing State.** Students learn the story of their state: its geography, its first peoples, and how it changed through the mission, rancho, Gold Rush, and statehood eras to the present.

Official text: https://www.cde.ca.gov/be/st/ss/

- **4.1** Demonstrate an understanding of the physical and human geographic features that define places and regions in California: latitude and longitude, the state's location in the Northern and Western Hemispheres, its regions (coast, valleys, mountains, deserts), and how geography and climate influence where and how people live.
- **4.2** Describe the social, political, cultural, and economic life and interactions among people of California from the pre-Columbian societies to the Spanish mission and Mexican rancho periods: the major nations of California Indians and how they adapted to and used the environment; early land and sea routes and the Spanish exploration; the mission system, its impact on California Indians, and their responses and resistance; Mexican independence, the rancho system, and daily life of the era.
- **4.3** Explain the economic, social, and political life in California from the establishment of the Bear Flag Republic through the Mexican-American War, the Gold Rush, and the granting of statehood: the routes and experiences of immigrants; the effects of the Gold Rush on settlements, daily life, politics, and the physical environment; the impact on California Indians; the diverse people who came and their experiences; and the Compromise of 1850 and California's admission as a free state.
- **4.4** Explain how California became an agricultural and industrial power, tracing the transformation of the economy and its political and cultural development since the 1850s: transportation and communication links (Pony Express, transcontinental railroad, and the Chinese and other workers who built it); immigration and migration (including the Dust Bowl and the Great Depression); conflicts and laws affecting immigrant groups; the development of water systems, agriculture, and industry (aerospace, technology, entertainment); and the contributions of key individuals and movements, including farmworker organizing.
- **4.5** Understand the structures, functions, and powers of the local, state, and federal governments as described in the U.S. Constitution: the purpose of the state constitution, the three branches of state government, the roles of local governments (cities, counties, tribal governments), and how California's government relates to the federal system.

## Teaching notes
- California's History-Social Science Framework discourages building mission models or reenacting mission life. Teach the mission period through multiple perspectives, including the impact on California Indians and their resistance, using primary sources.
- Present California Indian nations as diverse, sovereign, and present-day, not as a single group or only in the past.
- Gold Rush and immigration units can address the experiences of Chinese, Mexican, African American, and other communities, including discrimination they faced.
