# Grade 4 English Language Arts (California Common Core) - paraphrased

Official text: https://www.cde.ca.gov/be/st/ss/

## Reading: Literature (RL.4)
- **RL.4.1** Refer to details and examples in a text when explaining what it says explicitly and when drawing inferences.
- **RL.4.2** Determine a theme from details in the text; summarize the text.
- **RL.4.3** Describe in depth a character, setting, or event, drawing on specific details (thoughts, words, actions).
- **RL.4.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases, including those that allude to significant characters in mythology.
- **RL.4.5** Explain major differences between poems, drama, and prose, and refer to their structural elements (verse, rhythm, meter; casts of characters, settings, dialogue, stage directions).
- **RL.4.6** Compare and contrast the point of view from which different stories are narrated, including first- and third-person narrations.
- **RL.4.7** Connect the text of a story or drama with a visual or oral presentation of it.
- **RL.4.9** Compare and contrast the treatment of similar themes and topics and patterns of events in stories, myths, and traditional literature from different cultures.
- **RL.4.10** By year's end, read and comprehend grade 4-5 literature, with scaffolding as needed at the high end.

## Reading: Informational Text (RI.4)
- **RI.4.1** Refer to details and examples when explaining what a text says explicitly and when drawing inferences.
- **RI.4.2** Determine the main idea and explain how it is supported by key details; summarize the text.
- **RI.4.3** Explain events, procedures, ideas, or concepts in a historical, scientific, or technical text, including what happened and why.
- **RI.4.4** Determine the meaning of general academic and domain-specific words.
- **RI.4.5** Describe the overall structure (chronology, comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution) of events, ideas, or information.
- **RI.4.6** Compare and contrast a firsthand and secondhand account of the same event or topic.
- **RI.4.7** Interpret information presented visually, orally, or quantitatively (charts, graphs, diagrams, timelines, animations) and explain how it contributes to understanding.
- **RI.4.8** Explain how an author uses reasons and evidence to support particular points.
- **RI.4.9** Integrate information from two texts on the same topic to write or speak knowledgeably.
- **RI.4.10** By year's end, read and comprehend grade 4-5 informational texts, with scaffolding as needed at the high end.

## Foundational Skills (RF.4)
- **RF.4.3** Use combined knowledge of letter-sound correspondences, syllabication patterns, and morphology (roots and affixes) to read unfamiliar multisyllabic words accurately.
- **RF.4.4** Read with sufficient accuracy and fluency to support comprehension, using context to confirm or self-correct.

## Writing (W.4)
- **W.4.1** Opinion pieces: introduce a topic clearly, state an opinion, create an organizational structure grouping related ideas, provide reasons supported by facts and details, link opinion and reasons with words and phrases (for instance, in order to, in addition), and provide a concluding section.
- **W.4.2** Informative texts: introduce a topic and group related information in paragraphs and sections, include formatting, illustrations, and multimedia when useful, develop with facts, definitions, details, quotations, and examples, link ideas with words like another, for example, also, because, use precise language and domain-specific vocabulary, and provide a conclusion.
- **W.4.3** Narratives: orient the reader with a situation and narrator or characters, organize a natural event sequence, use dialogue and description, use transitional words, use concrete words and sensory details, and provide a conclusion.
- **W.4.4-9** Produce clear, coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose, and audience; plan, revise, and edit; use technology to publish and collaborate (including keyboarding a page in one sitting); conduct short research projects; gather information from print and digital sources, take notes, and provide a list of sources; draw evidence from literary or informational texts.
- **W.4.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames.

## Speaking & Listening (SL.4)
- **SL.4.1** Collaborative discussions: come prepared, follow rules and carry out assigned roles, pose and respond to questions, and review key ideas expressed.
- **SL.4.2-3** Paraphrase portions of a text read aloud or information presented in diverse media; identify the reasons and evidence a speaker provides.
- **SL.4.4-6** Report on a topic or text, tell a story, or recount an experience in an organized manner with appropriate facts and descriptive details, speaking clearly at an understandable pace; add audio recordings and visual displays; differentiate between contexts that call for formal English and situations where informal discourse is appropriate.

## Language (L.4)
- **L.4.1** Grammar: relative pronouns and relative adverbs; progressive verb tenses; modal auxiliaries (can, may, must); order adjectives conventionally; form and use prepositional phrases; produce complete sentences, correcting fragments and run-ons; correctly use frequently confused words (to/too/two, there/their). (California adds writing fluidly and legibly in cursive or joined italics.)
- **L.4.2** Conventions: capitalization; commas and quotation marks for direct speech and quotations; a comma before a coordinating conjunction in a compound sentence; spell grade-appropriate words correctly, consulting references.
- **L.4.3** Choose words and phrases to convey ideas precisely; choose punctuation for effect; differentiate formal and informal contexts.
- **L.4.4-6** Determine meanings using context, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and reference materials; explain similes and metaphors, idioms, adages, and proverbs; relate words to antonyms and synonyms; acquire and use grade-appropriate academic and domain-specific words.
